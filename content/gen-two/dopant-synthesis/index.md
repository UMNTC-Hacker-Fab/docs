# Spin-on Doping (SOD)

The Minnesota Nanofabrication Club (MNFC) is formulating a spin-on dopant (SOD) for use in doping a silicon semiconductor. This document explains the basic theory of doping, basics of SOD, and the overview for the formulation process currently underway. 

# What is doping?

Doping is the intentional introduction of impurities into a pure semiconductor material to change its electrical properties. In our case, we will be using P (Phosphorus) and B (Boron) as the chemical impurities and Si (silicon) as our semiconductor material. For electricity to flow through a material, it would need electrons that are willing to be influenced by an electric field. In the pure state of Si, it is a terrible conductor of electricity because its valence electrons are tightly locked in a crystalline lattice. This means that the electrons are not willing to move from their location, therefore not making any current or voltage. By strategically inserting a controlled amount and type of material into the crystal lattice, we can manipulate the concentration and movement of electrical charge carriers to unlock the conductivity under certain conditions. These can also be targeted to specific sections of the semiconductor to produce the end product, a chip. 

The process has 2 types of doped materials. N-type (negative) semiconductors and P-type (positive)semiconductors. N-type semiconductors (negative) are made by adding elements that donate an extra electron to the crystal lattice. In this case, we would have P with 5 valence electrons able to substitute a silicon atom, which requires 4 bonds, in the crystal lattice to donate 1 electron (5 valence electrons - 4 bonds = 1 extra electron). P-type semiconductors (positive) are made by adding elements that donate a hole to the crystal lattice. A hole refers to a place that an electron can fill from neighboring atoms. This would result in a silicon atom in the crystal lattice to have 3 bonds and an open position to accept an electron from a neighboring silicon atom. These “holes” can move the same way electrons can, but in the opposite direction as the electrons when placed in an electric field. In this case, we would have B with 3 valence electrons able to substitute a silicon atom, which still requires 4 bonds, in the crystal lattice to donate 1 hole–or alternatively you can think of it as taking 1 electron away from the crystal lattice–(3 valence electrons - 4 bonds = -1 electron or 1 hole).  



Doping is an important step in semiconductor manufacturing because it provides the precise control needed to create the core building blocks of all modern microelectronics. Without this process, pure silicon would behave as a weak insulator, completely incapable of handling electrical current, which wouldn’t allow for functions to be made from them. By strategically altering the electrical conductivity of specific locations, doping allows us to form p-n junctions, which act as a little gate that can start, stop, or amplify electrical signals. Some examples of devices that use these p-n junctions are diodes, light-emitting diodes, (LEDs), and MOSFET.
What is Spin-on Doping (SOD)?
Spin-on doping (SOD) is a method of introducing impurities into the semiconductor that combines a liquid coating with high heat baking to diffuse the dopant atoms mentioned above into the crystal lattice. In other words, the above information tells you about what the end result is, this section will tell you about how that situation happened using the starting ingredients. 

# Why SOD? 

SOD is one of the many types of industrial methods to get the dopant atoms into the substrate. Ion implantation is the most common method used in industry, which includes generating an ionized material via plasma and accelerating it into the silicon. This method is considered the most accurate method to give the best feature size for industrial applications. It would in theory be the best way of getting our doped silicon; however, for our purposes, it requires expensive machines–up to millions of dollars–and is dangerous to operate. Chemical Vapor Deposition is also a common industry method of doping. It works with dangerous gasses and requires more safety concerns and procedures. SOD is one of the safest methods that provides good enough results for our purposes in the club. It also doesn’t require additional expensive machines like ion implantation.

The process is outlined as follows: 
1. Prepare a solution for SOD. 
2. Use a spinner to get a silicon wafer spinning. This step is to make sure the liquid is uniformly distributed on the wafer, which is important for consistent results across the wafer in terms of diffusion testing and math. 
3. Place the wafer on a hot plate to solidify the aerogel layer into a solid structure. The aerogel will be discussed in a later section, as it isn’t required as a substance using traditional SOD from industry. 
4. Place the wafer into a tube furnace to diffuse the solid dopant layer into the wafer.
